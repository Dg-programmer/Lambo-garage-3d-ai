Lamborghini Huracán STO

Technical guide

How the six assemblies work, with notes for all thirty components. Exterior and cabin components come from the showroom mesh. The powertrain, suspension, and brake cutaways illustrate how those assemblies work.

Cofango

Front bodywork

The carbon-fiber Cofango integrates the nose, hood, and front fenders. Its openings manage air around the radiator and front wheels.

Cofango shell
The hood and fenders are molded into one lightweight piece. The cutaway shows that continuous shell as one component.
Radiator duct
The front duct guides incoming air through the radiator and out through the hood openings, helping cool the engine and reducing trapped air.
Fender louvers
Vents above the front tires let pressurized air escape the wheel housings, contributing to front downforce.
Front splitter
The low leading edge divides airflow. The air directed below the car feeds the underbody and rear diffuser.
Headlamp units
Compact LED housings combine forward illumination with the angular signature lighting visible at the front of the car.

Engine & transmission

V10 powertrain

The 5.2-liter naturally aspirated V10 sits behind the cockpit and sends power to the rear wheels. Lamborghini rates it at 640 CV at 8,000 rpm.

Intake plenum
The plenum and its runners distribute incoming air to both cylinder banks. The V10 draws air without a turbocharger.
Cylinder heads
Two rows of five cylinders form the V. The heads house the valves and camshafts that control intake and exhaust flow.
Engine block
The block supports the cylinders and crankshaft. The pistons turn combustion pressure into rotation through the connecting rods and crankshaft.
Transmission
The dual-clutch transmission changes the ratio between engine speed and rear-wheel speed. Its two clutches allow one ratio to be prepared while the other is engaged.
Exhaust headers
Curving pipes collect exhaust pulses from each bank and route them away from the engine toward the rear exhaust system.

CCM-R assembly

Wheel & brakes

The track-focused wheel surrounds a Brembo CCM-R carbon-ceramic brake disc. The system is designed to maintain consistent braking during repeated stops.

Tire
The tire transfers acceleration, cornering, and braking forces through its contact patch. The STO was developed with track-oriented Bridgestone tires.
20-inch rim
The thin spokes transmit load between the hub and rim while leaving airflow around the brake. Lamborghini offered 20-inch magnesium wheels for the STO.
CCM-R brake disc
The pads clamp this carbon-ceramic friction ring. Heat flows through the disc, and the brake ducts supply air to help cool the assembly.
Caliper and pads
Hydraulic pistons in the stationary caliper press the pads against both faces of the rotating disc.
Hub and central fastener
The hub locates the wheel on the axle and transmits torque. The center fastener secures the wheel to the hub.

Wing & diffuser

Rear aero

The slotted rear wing and underbody diffuser manage airflow at the rear. The wing has three manually adjustable settings for aerodynamic balance.

Main wing plane
The main airfoil directs airflow so the wing creates a downward force on the rear of the car at speed.
Adjustable wing element
Changing the element’s angle changes the balance of downforce and drag. The STO offers three manual wing settings.
Wing supports
The uprights hold the wing above the rear bodywork and transfer its aerodynamic load into the mounting structure.
End plates
The vertical panels at the wing tips help manage air moving around the ends of the wing.
Rear diffuser
The expanding underbody channels guide the airflow out behind the car. Vertical strakes keep the channels separated.

Driver controls

Cockpit

The driver’s controls and displays sit around a lightweight, race-inspired cabin. Lamborghini integrated the STO telemetry into the instruments and center display.

Steering wheel
The wheel transmits steering inputs and places driving controls within reach. The STO uses a fixed steering ratio.
Instrument display
The driver display presents speed, engine speed, and vehicle information. The connected telemetry system can also display track data.
Shift paddles
The paddles sit behind the steering wheel so the driver can request a shift without reaching for a separate lever.
Bucket seat
The shaped shell and side bolsters help keep the driver in place under braking and cornering. The model simplifies the padding and shell construction.
Restraint assembly
The belts and buckle hold the driver against the seat. The exhibit separates the restraint from the seat shell to show how they work together.

Spring, damper & links

Suspension

The suspension keeps the wheel controlled as it moves over the road. This cutaway explains the roles of the spring, damper, control arms, and wheel carrier.

Coil spring
The spring carries the supported load and compresses as the wheel moves upward. It returns that energy as it extends.
Damper body and shaft
The damper slows the spring’s motion and helps the wheel settle. Its shaft moves inside the body as the suspension travels.
Control arms
Upper and lower links guide the wheel carrier and determine how the wheel tilts as the suspension moves.
Wheel carrier
The upright connects the control arms to the hub and maintains the alignment of the wheel and brake assembly.
Anti-roll bar link
The link transfers suspension movement to the anti-roll bar. The bar resists the difference in travel between the two sides during cornering.
